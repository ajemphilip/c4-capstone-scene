COMPARISONS & CONTRASTS

Tensions Across Approaches

Not everything aligned. Productive tensions emerged that helped each team sharpen its approach by seeing what the others chose differently.

Digital vs. human-first: C2 and C3 built technology-led solutions; C1, C4, and C6 built relationship-led solutions. C5 sat between them — using design technology to serve a fundamentally human need. Each approach reached different people and created different types of value. Neither was wrong; but neither was sufficient alone.

Individual-scale vs. systemic-scale: C4 and C6 focused on paired, one-to-one relationships. C3 and C5 built infrastructure that could scale to thousands of users. C1 and C2 bridged the gap. Understanding why some problems demand intimacy and others demand reach was one of the cohort’s most important collective insights.

Short-term crisis vs. long-term prevention: C3 addressed immediate need (where is there a bed tonight?); C5 addressed structural cause (why is housing so expensive to build?). The urgency of crisis makes prevention feel abstract; the scale of prevention makes crisis feel too small. Holding both is genuinely hard.

INTERDISCIPLINARY CONNECTIONS

Where Disciplines Crossed

C4 is deliberately interdisciplinary. The six teams brought together students from technology, social work, architecture, community development, public health, and management. The most interesting insights came from where these disciplines — and the projects that embodied them — had to negotiate.

C2’s Housing Navigator and C3’s Resource Portal both discovered that the hard problem was not building the tool but maintaining the data — a fundamentally organizational and relational challenge, not a technical one. C5’s Affordable Housing Lab discovered that the hard problem was not designing the unit but navigating zoning law and community opposition — a political and management challenge, not an architectural one. C6’s Peer Support Circle discovered that the hard problem was not the curriculum but facilitator wellbeing — a human resources challenge, not a programme design one.

Every team eventually ran into the problem that their discipline was least equipped to solve. That is the point of interdisciplinary work.

INTEGRATIVE INSIGHT

What Only the Whole Can See

No single team’s solution is sufficient. A person experiencing homelessness needs safe housing (C5), a way to find and apply for it (C2), a map of the services around them (C3), connection with their community (C1), a mentor for the transition (C4), and support for their mental health through all of it (C6). These are not six projects — they are six chapters of one story. MANAGEMENT SECTION SYNTHESIS, MARCH 2025

The most important conclusion of the synthesis phase was that the six Launchpad projects are most powerful not as standalone interventions but as a coordinated ecosystem. The management section’s most significant contribution was making that ecosystem visible — and beginning the work of designing the connective tissue between them.
