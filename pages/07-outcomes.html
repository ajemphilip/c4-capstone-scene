EFFECTIVE STRATEGIES

What Worked

Co-design from the start. The teams that involved community members and people with lived experience from the first design sprint — not just as feedback-givers at the end, but as co-creators from the beginning — built more accurate, more trusted, and more adopted solutions. This was the single most consistent predictor of project quality.

Trauma-informed practice standards. Adopting a shared trauma-informed framework early in the programme changed how every team conducted research, designed interactions, and measured success. It also changed how students engaged with each other.

Weekly cross-team management syncs. A standing 90-minute weekly meeting among management students — not with Launchpad teams, but among the management section itself — created the synthesis layer that no team could build alone. It was often uncomfortable and always essential.

Shared Kanban framework. Using a common project management framework across all teams created visibility without uniformity. Teams could see each other’s blockers, share solutions, and coordinate deliverables without losing their distinct approaches.

Partnership stewardship protocols. Formalizing how each team managed its partner relationships — meeting cadences, decision-making authority, conflict escalation paths — prevented the kind of misalignment that tends to derail community partnerships in the last quarter of a programme.

START / STOP / CONTINUE

What We Would Change

START
  • Formal evaluation frameworks from project inception, not retrospectively
  • Cross-team learning exchanges earlier in Term 1
  • Lived-experience advisory panels with defined roles and compensation
  • Documented decision logs from Week 1
  • Structured handover planning with partners by Week 6
STOP
  • Siloed reporting without cross-team visibility
  • Over-engineering MVPs before any user validation
  • Treating partner feedback as validation rather than direction
  • Measuring success only at the end instead of iteratively
  • Assuming digital access for all user groups
CONTINUE
  • Community partner involvement at every decision point
  • Open-source documentation as a default, not an afterthought
  • Trauma-informed design standards across all teams
  • Weekly management synthesis sessions
  • Designing for the person least likely to access your solution
