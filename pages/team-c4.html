C4

Transition Mentorship Network

Pairing people moving into independent housing with mentors who have walked the same road — because lived experience is the most credible guide.

TEAM PHOTO — PLACEHOLDER
THEME

Project Theme

Pathway to Independence — building the social capital and practical skills needed to hold onto housing once it’s achieved, reducing the cycle of returns to homelessness.

PROBLEM

Problem Statement

Many individuals leaving shelters or transitional housing lack the informal support networks, soft skills, and day-to-day guidance that most housed people take for granted. Without ongoing connection, the first months of independent living are the most vulnerable — and the most likely to end in return to crisis.

WHY

Motivation

Crisis services address immediate needs; what comes next is often a cliff edge. A mentor who has navigated the same transition — the isolation, the bureaucracy, the small daily decisions — provides a form of support that no professional service can replicate.

CHALLENGE

Biggest Challenge

Designing a matching process that protects the dignity and privacy of participants while creating genuinely compatible pairs — not just logistically convenient ones. Preventing mentor burnout while maintaining consistent engagement required careful programme design from the ground up.

SOLUTION

Proposed Solution

A structured peer mentorship programme pairing individuals in new independent housing with trained mentors who have lived experience of homelessness. An algorithm matches pairs on shared goals, neighbourhood proximity, and availability. Dedicated case coordinators provide backstop support and safeguarding for both parties.

OUTCOME

Outcome

Eight mentor–mentee pairs formed and active. Six-month pilot launched in partnership with a local supportive housing provider. 100% of mentees reported feeling “more confident about staying housed” at the three-month check-in. Zero programme exits due to housing loss in the pilot cohort.

SDGs

Target SDG Goals

SDG 1 · No Poverty SDG 10 · Reduced Inequalities SDG 17 · Partnerships for Goals
LESSON

Most Important Lesson Learned

Lived experience is a professional asset, not a personal detail. The mentors in this programme were more effective, more trusted, and more insightful than any external expert we consulted. Designing around that truth changes everything about how the programme works.

FUTURE

Future Direction

Develop a formal accreditation pathway so mentors can convert their programme participation into a recognised professional credential. Scale to 40 active pairs across three partner organisations. Publish the programme model as an open-source toolkit for replication in other cities.
