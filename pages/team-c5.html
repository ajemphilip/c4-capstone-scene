C5

Affordable Housing Design Lab

Re-imagining what “affordable” looks like — proving that dignity and cost do not have to be in conflict.

TEAM PHOTO — PLACEHOLDER
THEME

Project Theme

Innovative Housing Solutions — using design thinking and modular construction to bring down the cost and delivery time of dignified housing units without sacrificing liveability.

PROBLEM

Problem Statement

Traditional construction models are too slow and too expensive to keep pace with the housing gap. Meanwhile, the affordable units that do get built are often stigmatised by poor design, poor location choices, and a cost-cutting logic that treats future residents as an afterthought rather than the client.

WHY

Motivation

Design innovation in market-rate housing regularly achieves dramatic efficiency gains. Those same techniques — modular prefabrication, adaptive reuse, infill development — are almost never applied at the affordable end of the market. We wanted to close that gap with actual drawings and numbers, not just theory.

CHALLENGE

Biggest Challenge

Zoning bylaws and building codes were designed for conventional construction and actively resist innovation. Community opposition to non-traditional housing formats — the “not in my backyard” reflex — added a political layer to every design decision. Getting a proposal to the feasibility stage required navigating both.

SOLUTION

Proposed Solution

A modular micro-unit design system deployable on underutilised urban infill lots, including laneways and surface parking. Units arrive largely pre-built, with full plumbing, insulation, and accessibility features. A “kit of parts” approach allows configurations from single-occupancy studios to four-person family flats on the same structural chassis.

OUTCOME

Outcome

Three architectural prototypes developed to schematic design stage. Two municipal pre-consultations completed with favourable responses. A published feasibility study showing a 38% cost reduction per unit compared to conventional purpose-built affordable housing in the same market.

SDGs

Target SDG Goals

LESSON

Most Important Lesson Learned

Good design is invisible. The best affordable unit is one whose resident eventually forgets the word “affordable” — because it simply feels like home. Every design decision that reminded the occupant of their economic circumstances was a failure, not a feature.

FUTURE

Future Direction

Secure a partnership with a non-profit developer to build a 20-unit pilot on city-owned infill land. Publish the chassis design under an open licence so other municipalities and developers can adapt it. Pursue a provincial grant to fund a full construction prototype for load testing and code compliance review.
