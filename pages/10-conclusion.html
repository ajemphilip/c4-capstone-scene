Conclusion

An integrative management reflection — what eight months inside a real problem taught us about what management is actually for.

THE MANAGEMENT LENS

What This Capstone Was Really About

Management education is full of cases. Cases are clean: the problem is contained in the document, the data is already organized, and the protagonist’s decision is the only variable. C4 was the opposite. The problem was not contained. The data was not organized. And the protagonist was not a single executive but thirty students, six teams, seven organizations, and hundreds of community members — each with different definitions of success.

This is not a failure of the programme design. It is the point of it. Real management — the kind that matters for society, not just for firms — operates in exactly this kind of complexity. Learning to find traction in that complexity, without pretending it is simpler than it is, is the most important thing a management education can do.

CORE ARGUMENT

Relationship Architecture

The management challenge of this capstone was not technical problem-solving. It was relationship architecture — building the trust infrastructure that allows solutions to work, the coordination infrastructure that allows teams to learn from each other, and the narrative infrastructure that allows a portfolio of six projects to tell a coherent story. MANAGEMENT SECTION INTEGRATIVE REFLECTION, APRIL 2025

Every project management framework in the curriculum — stakeholder mapping, risk registers, Kanban boards, impact measurement — is useful. But these tools only work inside a container of trust. The management section’s most important work was building that container: between teams, between students and partners, and between the programme and the communities it served.

TO FUTURE COHORTS

A Note to What Comes Next

If you are reading this as a future C4 management student, you are inheriting something real. Six working prototypes, seven partner relationships, thirty people’s worth of hard-won knowledge about what homelessness looks like from the inside of an attempt to address it. This is not a blank canvas. It is a foundation.

The most useful thing we can tell you is this: the programme will feel very large and very unclear for longer than is comfortable. The clarity comes from the work, not before it. Start the work early. Stay in contact with your partners. Talk to people with lived experience before you talk to experts. Write everything down as if someone else will carry it forward. Because they will.

We made mistakes. Some of them are documented in this portfolio; others are in the decisions that are hard to trace back. Learn from the ones we named, and find the ones we didn’t. That is what a cohort does for the one after it.

CLOSING

Not a Record of Completion

This portfolio is not a record of completion. It is an opening move — the first honest account of what it took to begin addressing homelessness from inside a university, with real partners, under real constraints, in real time. The problem is not solved. The work is not done. But something real was built, and that something has a foundation now that it did not have before. C4 MANAGEMENT SECTION, YORK UNIVERSITY, 2025
