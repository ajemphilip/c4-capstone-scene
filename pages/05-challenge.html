Challenge & Themes

The question we kept returning to, and the six thematic territories it opened.

CENTRAL CHALLENGE

The Design Question

“How might we design sustainable, dignified, and community-centred solutions to address homelessness in the York Region — solutions that meet people where they are, build on their strengths, and create pathways that last beyond any single programme?” C4 COHORT DESIGN CHALLENGE, SEPTEMBER 2024

This challenge was not assigned — it was co-developed with partner organizations and people with lived experience over the first four weeks of the programme. The specificity of “sustainable,” “dignified,” and “community-centred” emerged from those conversations as non-negotiable constraints, not optional aspirations.

CONTEXT

Why Homelessness, Why Now

Homelessness in Canada is not a niche social problem — it is a systems failure visible in every major city, increasingly in suburban and regional communities like York Region. Approximately 235,000 people experience homelessness annually across Canada. In York Region alone, point-in-time counts consistently undercount the scale of hidden homelessness: people couch-surfing, staying in overcrowded housing, or sleeping in their vehicles.

The causes are interlocking: housing affordability, mental health under-investment, inadequate transitional support, social isolation, and a service system that too often requires people to prove their need rather than simply meet it. C4 chose not to simplify these causes into a single solvable problem. Instead, it assigned six teams to work on six different entry points into the same system.

THEMES

Six Thematic Territories

Each theme represents a distinct dimension of homelessness that a Launchpad team explored in depth. Together they map the full complexity of the challenge.

SIGNIFICANCE

Relevance & Scope

These six themes were not chosen arbitrarily. They emerged from two sources: the literature on housing stability (which consistently identifies social connection, mental health, and resource access as the three strongest predictors of long-term housing retention) and the partner organizations themselves, who identified the gaps their programmes could not fill on their own.

The themes also reflect a deliberate span from immediate-crisis response (C3) to long-term structural change (C5) — ensuring C4 did not focus only on downstream symptoms while ignoring upstream causes, or vice versa.

SDG ALIGNMENT

United Nations Sustainable Development Goals

The C4 cohort mapped its work explicitly to the UN’s Sustainable Development Goals. Across six teams, the portfolio collectively addresses six SDGs.

SDG 1 · No Poverty SDG 3 · Good Health & Wellbeing SDG 10 · Reduced Inequalities SDG 11 · Sustainable Cities & Communities SDG 16 · Peace, Justice & Strong Institutions SDG 17 · Partnerships for the Goals

SDG 11 (Sustainable Cities & Communities) and SDG 10 (Reduced Inequalities) appear across all six team projects, reflecting the shared understanding that homelessness is both a housing problem and an equity problem — inseparable in cause and in solution.
