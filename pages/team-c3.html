The Project

Goals & Outcomes

What We Set Out To Achieve

Team C3 set out to challenge the stigma faced by people experiencing homelessness and to give partner organizations a shared space to coordinate. The first goal was to shift public understanding by bringing communities, organizations, and people with lived experience together to learn from one another directly rather than rely on assumptions. The second was to address fragmentation among siloed organizations through shared infrastructure to promote events, coordinate outreach, and gather feedback. The team achieved two tangible outcomes, a fully designed community event model and a working platform prototype with event discovery, role based administration, and a feedback system, both shaped and validated through ongoing partner feedback. Together they reflect the team’s broader goal of treating homelessness not only as a housing issue but as a challenge of community integration and belonging.

Strategies & Processes

How We Made It Happen

Team C3’s strategy centred on direct research, stakeholder engagement, and iterative feedback. Early rapid research and a site visit to BetterStreet grounded the problem in real community dynamics, while partner conversations with HICC, YSB, and Trek for Teens shaped the design. The team’s process relied on Miro boards for discussion, a five whys analysis to find root causes, and short sprints with regular retrospectives to keep refining. Communication stayed open throughout, with WhatsApp chats and free-flowing discussions where every member contributed both online and in person. Two ideas emerged, a digital platform and a community event, and rather than choose between them the team adopted a strategy of combining the two into a single hybrid solution shaped directly by partner feedback. Across many disciplines, members divided roles by strength, and this process of ongoing dialogue with partners and the teaching team carried the project to a shared outcome.

Key Achievements

What We Accomplished

Team C3 produced a working digital platform prototype, deployed live, with event discovery and registration for community members, a role based admin panel for organizers, and a structured anonymized feedback system. Alongside it, the team developed a low cost, adaptable community event model and reusable guidelines that partners can shape to their own neighbourhood and publish through the platform. Both were designed to center people experiencing homelessness, pairing dialogue and public education that challenges stigma with shared infrastructure that helps partner organizations coordinate. Partners saw the prototype at demo day and confirmed event discovery and feedback as the highest value features, validating the core of what the team set out to build.

Critical Evaluation

Looking Back Critically

Looking back, the team is proud of the foundation it built, while recognising there is still room to grow. Much of that confidence comes from the research gathered and the encouraging feedback offered by partners along the way, which consistently affirmed that the project was heading in a meaningful direction. Still, the team does wish it had been able to run the event within the course timeline, since its impact remains something anticipated rather than something it has yet had the chance to demonstrate. The compressed schedule made this difficult, since partners need adequate notice and meaningful community turnout takes time to build, and while several venues were considered around campus, with partners, and at leisure parks, none were able to accommodate the needs of the event given the time and circumstances. Part of this came down to a broad scope, which is something the team reflected on, since a tighter focus may have made the event easier to place and deliver. Even so, the experience offered a great deal of learning about working across disciplines and listening closely to the people the project was meant to serve, and in the end that learning feels just as valuable as the deliverables themselves.

Recommendations

Our Takeaways and Recommendations

Team C3 came to see equity as mutual responsiveness to difference rather than sameness, which reshaped how they designed feedback for community members and for people experiencing homelessness. The team also learned that lasting impact starts with getting the foundation right and building real relationships, not just designing something that looks good on paper. Building on these takeaways, the team’s main recommendation is to collaborate on the event with partners to publish and put the guidelines to the test in a real setting. This recommendation would call for narrowing the target audience and allowing more lead time so partners and sponsors can take part. The platform would then collect feedback from those who attend, and analyzing it would show whether the model delivers real value, help refine the guidelines, and reveal to what extent the event meaningfully shifts understanding of homelessness.

The Journey

Starting Point

Where It All Began

Team C3’s theme was Community Integration, applied to homelessness and housing insecurity. The initial angle was broad, framed loosely around young adults and shaped by early partner contact with BetterStreet, which surfaced the challenge of engaging residents who hold NIMBY attitudes. From the start the team was drawn to two threads, shifting public understanding of homelessness and giving fragmented organizations a way to coordinate. That early framing pointed toward what became a paired event and platform, though the specific audience and scope were still open questions. What held steady throughout was the team’s commitment to keeping people experiencing homelessness at the center of the work.

Major Developments

How Our Thinking Evolved

Feedback steadily sharpened the work. An early decision removed a social worker facing portal, refocusing the platform on event organizers and community members. Team C3 explored and set aside several ideas, a resource directory that duplicated platforms like 211, a community forum that risked becoming hard to moderate, and an organizer only dashboard that left out the community. A midway review flagged scope drift, which the team tightened immediately. When BetterStreet warned a feedback channel could become chaos, they pivoted to structured anonymized surveys the same day. The event itself moved through online, on campus, and neighbourhood versions as constraints became clearer.

Challenges

The Walls We Had to Climb

Time was the steepest wall. Shifting the social stigma around homelessness takes patient, in person dialogue, but partners and sponsors need real lead time to take part, so the compressed timeline left Team C3 no room to fully stage the event or hear back from them. The platform faced the same squeeze, with too little time to field test the app with the community it was built for. Carrying two connected projects across many disciplines also brought communication challenges, since ideas rooted in one field can be hard to share. The team met this by dividing tasks to strengths and talking constantly.

Lessons Learned

What We’d Do Differently

Looking back, Team C3 felt the work could have been tighter with a single, united solution rather than two parallel projects, which stretched coordination and communication across the team. The clearest process lesson was staying more on top of the Miro boards and keeping a dedicated task outline alongside them, since some messages were lost in the flow of the group chat and a shared outline would have brought more clarity. The team also learned the value of committing to scope early, since the parallel approach made it harder to give either solution full attention. More broadly, they would build in the lead time that partners and sponsors need, so the event could be properly tested rather than designed for a window too short to see it through.

The Legacy

Future Direction & Continuation Feasibility

Looking to the Future

With more time in the future, events could run at a fuller scale, reach the audiences who can help end homelessness, and open dialogue between the different sides. Team C3’s project could grow each event to neighbourhood scale, add multilingual support for newcomers and accessibility features for users with disabilities, link to municipal housing resource directories, and move toward longer term deployment with partners. The event model is designed to be repeatable and replicable across neighbourhoods, and the guidelines let partners host their own. The platform itself is built to feasibly sustain partnerships beyond the project, giving the work a realistic path to continue.

Responsible Interest Holders

Who We’d Work With

People with lived experience remain central, both as voices in the events and as participants whose feedback shapes the services offered to them. The natural interest holders alongside them are the community partners already involved with Team C3, BetterStreet, YSB, HICC, and Trek for Teens, who shaped the project and could carry it forward. The team consistently gravitated toward BetterStreet in particular, whose frank and grounded feedback steered the project at almost every turn. Frontline organizations such as shelters, outreach teams, and community support agencies could use the platform as a digital navigator in their daily work. Local sponsors and businesses could support events, while municipal services and government bodies could connect through resource directory integration.

Barriers

Future Mountains to Climb

The clearest barrier is sustained engagement. Real impact on stigma or housing outcomes would need longer term deployment, ongoing partner commitment, and broader community adoption beyond the prototype. Events depend on partners and sponsors who need lead time and concrete plans to rally behind, and outreach can be slow. Keeping the platform’s resource and event listings current relies on participating organizations continuing to update them. Questions of ownership, maintenance, and funding remain open. Narrowing the audience and proving value through a fully run event are the next hurdles for Team C3.
