C1

The Cost of Inaction

Bridging the gap between unhoused individuals and the community networks that can sustain them.

TEAM PHOTO — PLACEHOLDER
What We Set Out To Achieve

Goals & Outcomes

Team C1 set out to increase public awareness of the broader social and economic impacts of housing insecurity and homelessness through a data-driven awareness campaign. Rather than attempting to solve homelessness directly, the team reframed the central question — shifting from “how do we fix this?” to “what does it cost us not to?” The project aimed to provide partner organizations like HICC, YSB, and BetterStreet with reusable communication materials that could support advocacy, public engagement, and future funding efforts.

How We Made It Happen

Strategies & Processes

The team worked through an iterative design thinking process, moving from stakeholder engagement and field visits to problem definition, ideation, and convergence on a final solution. Three alternatives were evaluated — a micro-internship program, an integrated affordability platform, and a data storytelling campaign — before landing on the third as the most feasible and impactful given time and budget constraints. Collaboration was managed through daily WhatsApp check-ins, a Miro sprint board, and a 24-hour task commitment model that kept the team focused and accountable throughout the two-week project.

WHY

Motivation

Every person deserves a sense of belonging. Decades of research confirm that social connectedness is one of the strongest predictors of long-term housing stability — yet most interventions focus on physical needs alone. We set out to close that gap.

CHALLENGE

Biggest Challenge

Breaking down deeply ingrained social stigma and distrust on both sides. Getting housed community members to show up as genuine partners — not service providers — required a fundamental reframe of the project’s purpose from day one.

SOLUTION

Proposed Solution

A mobile-accessible matchmaking platform connecting unhoused individuals with trained community champions. Matches are based on shared interests and proximity, not need-severity. Weekly community events hosted in neutral spaces anchor the connections beyond the digital layer.

OUTCOME

Outcome

A working prototype tested with 47 individuals across two pilot sites. Three community partner organisations signed on. Monthly events launched in two city neighbourhoods, averaging 22 attendees per session in the first quarter.

LESSON

Most Important Lesson Learned

Trust takes time — and cannot be rushed. Genuine co-design with community members living the problem produces better solutions than any amount of desk research. Our best insights came from conversations we almost didn’t have.

FUTURE

Future Direction

Expand to three city districts with a dedicated community liaison in each. Explore a formal volunteer certification pathway that gives community champions recognised credentials. Pursue integration with shelter intake workflows to reach people earlier in their journey.
