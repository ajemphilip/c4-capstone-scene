C6

Peer Support Circle

Human connection as the foundation of recovery — structured peer support that meets people where they are, without replacing the clinical care they also need.

TEAM PHOTO — PLACEHOLDER
THEME

Project Theme

Mental Health & Wellbeing — addressing the trauma, isolation, and untreated mental health challenges that are both drivers of and barriers to escaping homelessness.

PROBLEM

Problem Statement

Mental health challenges are deeply intertwined with homelessness, yet trauma-informed peer support is severely underfunded and unevenly distributed. Clinical services have long waitlists and high thresholds for entry. The gap between “not in crisis” and “receiving regular mental health support” is where most people fall through.

WHY

Motivation

Human connection is one of the most powerful healing forces available — and one of the least expensive. Shared experience between peers creates a form of understanding that no professional credential can substitute. We wanted to build a structured container for that connection to happen safely.

CHALLENGE

Biggest Challenge

Building a genuinely safe, trauma-informed environment without crossing the line into clinical territory. Keeping boundaries clear — for participants, facilitators, and referring agencies — while still creating the psychological safety that makes the circles effective required extensive facilitation training and clear protocols from the start.

SOLUTION

Proposed Solution

A facilitated peer support circle model meeting weekly in shelter and community spaces. Facilitators are trained in trauma-informed practices and hold lived experience of homelessness or mental health challenges. Each circle has 6–10 participants, a clear agenda structure, and warm referral pathways to clinical services when needed.

OUTCOME

Outcome

Weekly circles running continuously for four months across two sites. 31 regular participants. Nine warm referrals to clinical mental health services made. Facilitator retention at 100% through the pilot period. Participant-reported sense of belonging increased from 2.1 to 4.4 out of 5 at the three-month mark.

SDGs

Target SDG Goals

SDG 3 · Good Health & Wellbeing SDG 10 · Reduced Inequalities SDG 16 · Peace, Justice & Strong Institutions
LESSON

Most Important Lesson Learned

You cannot pour from an empty cup. Supporting facilitator wellbeing — with supervision, peer support of their own, and genuine recognition — is as important as supporting participants. Programmes that ignore this burn through facilitators and lose what makes them work.

FUTURE

Future Direction

Develop a train-the-trainer model to equip local facilitators at five new sites, reducing dependence on central coordination. Create a facilitator handbook and publish it under open licence. Pursue integration with the city’s mental health and addictions pathway so circles are formally recognised as a first-contact option.
